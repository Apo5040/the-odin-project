A cherry Clafoutis is a famous, traditional French country dessert hailing from the Limousin region, highlighting the sweetness of seasonal cherries.

It consists of juicy fresh cherries blanketed in a rich, flan-like custard batter made of milk, eggs, flour, and sugar. Baked until beautifully puffed and golden brown, it offers a comforting texture that perfectly balances the tartness of the fruit.

Ingredients

  • 600g of cherries
  • 40g of semi-salted butter + 20g for the mold
  • 4 eggs
  • 20cl of milk
  • 100g of flour
  • 60g of powdered sugar
  • 1 packet of vanilla sugar
  • 1 pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Preheat the oven to 210°C (410°F, thermostat 7). Wash the cherries quickly under cold water, pit them, and drain them. Personally, I remove the pits, but the traditional recipe calls for leaving them in, so it's up to you whether you leave them or not...
  2. Melt the 40g of butter in a small, thick-bottomed saucepan. In a large bowl, mix the flour, sugar, salt, and vanilla sugar. Incorporate the eggs one by one, then pour the milk in gradually while continuing to mix. Add the melted butter.
  3. Generously butter the baking dish, arrange the cherries, then pour the clafoutis batter over them.
  4. Bake for 10 minutes at 210°C (410°F), then lower the temperature to 180°C (350°F) and bake for another 20 minutes.
  5. Serve the clafoutis cold or lukewarm, dusted with powdered sugar.
